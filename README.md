# Wave Field

**Wave Field** is a deterministic two-player strategy game played on a 7x7 board
generated by the pieces themselves. Every pawn, rook, spy, and king emits spatial
wave components. Their combined field divides the board into Red, Neutral, and
Blue territory.

This repository is a live, playable application of the current rules:

**[Play Wave Field](https://rollingstorms.github.io/wave-field/)**

## Objective

Win by **spectrally trapping the opposing king**: place it on hostile territory
with no legal escape to friendly or neutral territory.

A stable king with no available moves does not lose. The king must be both
unstable and unable to escape.

## The Field

Every piece contributes a signed wave to every square:

- Red pieces contribute positively.
- Blue pieces contribute negatively.
- Contributions from all pieces are added together.

The resulting value determines the territory:

| Field value | Territory |
|---|---|
| Greater than zero | Red |
| Zero | Neutral |
| Less than zero | Blue |

Moving a piece changes its wave origin, so one move can reshape territory across
the entire board.

Every basis wave halves once per square ring: `1`, `1/2`, `1/4`, `1/8`, and so
on. Coefficients and strengths are integers, so combined field totals are always
dyadic fractions whose denominators are powers of two. A sum may be `3/8` or
`5/16`, but never `1/3` or `1/5`.

## Pieces

Each player begins with two pawns, two rooks, one spy, and one king. Blue moves
first.

| Piece | Wave components | Strength | Movement |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Pawn | 1 | 1 | One adjacent square |
| Rook | 2 | 2 | One adjacent square |
| Spy | 3 | 1 | One adjacent square; ignores territory |
| King | 4 | 2 | One adjacent square |

Adjacent movement includes all eight surrounding squares, including diagonals.
Despite their familiar names, pieces do not use chess movement.

## Turn

During a turn, a player may:

1. Tune their wave components any number of times.
2. Move one piece.

Moving a piece ends the turn. Tuning does not.

A player may not make a tuning change or move that leaves their own king
spectrally trapped.

## Tuning

Each component can be set to:

- `+` for a positive wave;
- `0` to disable it;
- `-` for a negative wave.

The number of active `+` and `-` components combined cannot exceed that piece
type's strength. For example, the king has four available components but may
activate only two at once; the spy has three available components but may
activate only one.

Tuning is shared by piece type. Both of a player's rooks always use that
player's rook settings, while the opposing player controls a separate set.

## Movement and Territory

All destinations must be on the board, adjacent, and unoccupied. Pieces cannot
capture by moving onto another piece; both friendly and opposing pieces block
the square.

- Red pawns, rooks, and kings may enter Red or Neutral territory.
- Blue pawns, rooks, and kings may enter Blue or Neutral territory.
- Spies may enter any unoccupied adjacent square.

## Instability

A non-spy piece standing on hostile territory is **unstable**.

If a player has an unstable non-king piece when they move, they must move one of
their unstable pieces to a stable legal square. An unstable piece with no legal
escape is removed, and the field is recalculated because its wave disappears.
This can trigger further changes across the board.

If several friendly pieces are unstable, moving one rescues only that piece.
After the move, every other previously unstable piece that remains on hostile
territory is removed. A threatened piece also survives if the rescue move
changes the field enough to stabilize its square.

Spies are never unstable and are never removed by hostile territory.

## Victory

A king is trapped when:

1. It occupies hostile territory.
2. It has no unoccupied adjacent square in friendly or neutral territory.

When a move produces that state for the opposing king, the moving player wins.
Kings are not captured or removed.

## Starting Position

The opening formation is rotationally symmetric:

```text
        Blue
    . . R K R . .
    . . P S P . .
    . . . . . . .
    . . . . . . .
    . . . . . . .
    . . P S P . .
    . . R K R . .
         Red
```

`P` = pawn, `R` = rook, `S` = spy, `K` = king.

## The Live Rules Demo

The demo includes live wave-pattern thumbnails, a per-square piece-type
contribution overlay, legal-move indicators, instability warnings, undo and
restart, high-contrast territory markers, and a developer mode for inspecting
the raw field, piece contributions, mobility, and editable wave definitions.

The implementation is deterministic: the same position and component settings
always produce the same field and legal moves.

## Run Locally

```bash
npm install
npm run dev
```

Use `npm test` to run the rules test suite and `npm run build` to create the
production build.
